Map Browser

A Mac web browser that looks and works like Arc, built to go easy on your battery.

Why Map Browser

Arc's layout and feel

Vertical tabs, favorites, pinned tabs, a command bar instead of a new-tab page, swipeable spaces, and light and dark themes that follow your Mac.

Gentle on battery

The window doesn't redraw when nothing changes, tabs from your last session load only when you open them, and background services that browse on their own are off.

Ads and trackers blocked

Pages load fewer scripts, so ad-heavy sites use less battery, CPU and data.

Separate spaces, separate accounts

Each space keeps its own cookies and site data, so the same site can be signed in to different accounts in different spaces.

Pages keep running

Pages you've opened stay alive when you switch tabs or spaces, so chat and mail keep receiving messages.

Updates itself

New versions download in the background. Click Restart when Map Browser says one is ready, or it installs the next time you quit.

Installing

  1. Open the downloaded disk image and drag Map Browser onto Applications.
  2. Open Map Browser from Applications. Map Browser isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time, macOS says it can't check it for malicious software. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to Map Browser, then confirm. On macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click Map Browser in Applications and choose Open instead.

macOS may also ask whether Map Browser can use “Chromium Safe Storage” in your keychain, on the first launch and after updates. It's the key that protects your saved logins and cookies; allow it to stay signed in to sites.

Getting around

⌘T opens the command bar: type an address or a search, or find an open tab, a page from your history or an action. ⌘S hides the sidebar, ⌃1–⌃9 switch spaces, and ⌘, opens the settings. Help › Keyboard Shortcuts lists the rest.
